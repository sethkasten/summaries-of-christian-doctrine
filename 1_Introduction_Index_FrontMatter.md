# The Gospel Collects of Veit Dietrich (1548)

**The German text of the *Summaria Christlicher Lehr*, with the collects of *The Lutheran Hymnary* (1913) revised against it, and the remaining collects newly translated in the same idiom**

Every Gospel collect in Dietrich's *Summaria* is given here in its original German, followed by its English. Where *The Lutheran Hymnary* (TLHy) has the prayer, its English has been checked clause by clause against the German, corrected where it departs from it, and printed as **Revised TLHy**, with every change listed beneath it. Where TLHy lacks the prayer, a **New translation** in TLHy's idiom is given instead. The index in §3 lists every collect in church-year order and links to it.

## 1. Source and scope

**Source.** Veit Dietrich, *Summaria Christlicher lehr/für das junge volck/Was auß eim yeden Sontags Euāgelio zu mercken sey/Sampt angehenckten Gebeten. durch Vitum Dietrich. M.D.XLVIII.* [Summaries of Christian Doctrine for the Young People: What Is to Be Noted from Each Sunday's Gospel, Together with Appended Prayers. By Vitus Dietrich. 1548.] Google Books digitization, 306 PDF pages. The book has signatures but no page numbers, so every page reference below is to the **PDF page** of the scan (PDF p. 3 = title page). The colophon (PDF p. 304) reads *Gedruckt zu Nürmberg/durch Johañ vom Berg/vnd Vlrich Newber.* [Printed at Nuremberg by Johann vom Berg and Ulrich Neuber.]

PDF pp. 1–2 are Google's digitization notices. The book opens with the title page (PDF p. 3; its verso, p. 4, is blank) and Dietrich's dedicatory letter to Johann Neudörffer, dated 2 September 1546 (pp. 5–12). The temporale runs PDF pp. 13–228 (First Sunday in Advent through the Twenty-fifth Sunday after Trinity, with St. Stephen's Day placed after Christmas). The sanctorale, headed *Summaria Christlicher lehr auß den Euangelien der fürnembsten Fest durchs Jar* [Summaries of Christian doctrine from the Gospels of the principal feasts through the year], runs pp. 229–296 (St. Andrew through Sts. Simon and Jude). After the sanctorale come Dietrich's address *Dem Christlichen Leser* [To the Christian Reader] (pp. 297–298), two appended prayers (pp. 299–303) and the colophon (p. 304).

**Scope.** This edition gives every prayer that Stratman marks (S), meaning it comes from the *Summaria*: 80 numbered collects. Because [86] and [87] are the same prayer printed twice, there are 79 distinct texts. Bracketed numbers are Stratman's throughout.

- **54 collects are in TLHy.** Each is printed as *Revised TLHy*, with its changes listed. They include [10], which TLHy prints at Quinquagesima and which is restored here to the Sunday after New Year, and Maundy Thursday, where TLHy's collect ([33]) proves to be a free adaptation of Dietrich's [32].
- **26 collects are not in TLHy.** Each is given in a *New translation* in TLHy's idiom. Among them is Dietrich's own Quinquagesima collect [23].
- **TLHy's other collects**, which do not come from Dietrich, are given in Appendix A, so that every TLHy day can be found.
- The front matter (title page and dedicatory letter) precedes Part I, and Part III gives the two prayers appended at the end of the book.

## 2. Conventions

### 2.1 Transcription

- **Semi-diplomatic.** Original spelling, capitalization and punctuation are kept, including the virgule (/). Abbreviations stay as printed: vñ = vnd, dz = das, and a bar or tilde over a letter stands for a following m or n (lebē = leben, stim̃ = stimm). Long ſ and r rotunda (ꝛ) are normalized to s and r. Superscript-e vowels are given as ä, ö, ü. Words broken at a line end are joined without comment.
- **Decorated initials.** The initial is printed as part of the first word (HErr, HERR, ALmechtiger, O Jesu).
- **j-forms.** The printer sets the pronoun forms *jn, jm, jhn, jren* with a descending j, distinct from the preposition *in*. This was checked at 400 dpi and is followed here.
- **Misprints and damage.** Misprints are kept and marked [sic], with the intended reading. Damaged type is noted where it occurs.
- **Front matter.** The title page is given line by line. In the dedicatory letter, [p. 6] and the like mark where a new PDF page begins; when a page ends inside a divided word, the marker follows that word. Catchwords and signatures are omitted, and ꝛc., the sign for *et cetera*, is kept as printed.
- **Checking.** Every transcription was read from the page images at 200 dpi, and doubtful points were checked at 400 dpi. The scan's OCR layer was used only to find pages and, afterwards, to cross-check that no words or lines had been skipped.

### 2.2 TLHy's idiom, and how it has been revised

Every TLHy collect that comes from the *Summaria* was set beside Dietrich's German and compared clause by clause. The comparison shows how TLHy worked:

1. **Register and syntax.** TLHy uses Thee/Thou/Thy/Thine, -est/-eth verbs and capitalized divine pronouns. It shapes each prayer as a collect: invocation, a relative clause ("who hast…"), a colon, then the petition ("We beseech Thee…"). Scripture echoes are given in King James wording.
2. **Plural for singular.** TLHy recasts Dietrich's first-person-singular prayers in the plural (Epiphany 2; Trinity 11, 12, 19 and 20).
3. **Conclusions.** TLHy adds its full conclusion to every prayer, whether Dietrich ends with no conclusion at all, with a short "durch Christum…" (e.g., Easter 1, Trinity 8), or with a Trinitarian close of his own (Epiphany 4; Trinity 22 and 25).
4. **Free paraphrase.** TLHy often paraphrases. It drops clauses (Epiphany 4's "to hear our prayer in all temptation and need"; Trinity 13's "and sooner forsake all things"), adds others (Maundy Thursday's "remember Thine agony and death"; Trinity 8's prayer to "send us now and ever pious pastors"), and at times reverses the sense (Trinity 18 blames "the weakness of our flesh and blood," where Dietrich says that flesh and blood are too *strong* in us). At Maundy Thursday it readdresses Dietrich's prayer to Christ.

**How TLHy is revised here.**

- *Kept:* TLHy's register, syntax, collect shape and "We beseech Thee"; its settled equivalents wherever they render the German without loss (table below); King James wording wherever Dietrich's German follows the same verse ("overcharged with surfeiting and drunkenness, and cares of this life," Advent 2; "an honest and good heart," Sexagesima; "besides women and children," Trinity 7); and TLHy's conclusions (§2.4).
- *Corrected:* omissions are restored; additions are removed; changed sense is given literally; Dietrich's address, person and number are restored, and for the singular prayers a plural "congregational form" is supplied, as TLHy's practice requires; *dein liebes wort* is "Thy dear word," not "Thy holy word"; and "Holy Ghost" replaces "Holy Spirit" (§2.3). Slips in the Orthodox Lutheran Ministerium's web text of TLHy, such as "Holy Gaost" or a closing "Amen,", are corrected and noted.
- *Recorded:* every change is listed under **Changes from TLHy**, in the form "TLHy wording" → "revised wording," followed by the German that requires it.

The new translations follow the same rules, so that the two kinds of text can stand side by side. The prose of the front matter is translated in formal modern English instead, with Scripture still in King James wording.

### 2.3 "Holy Ghost"

"Holy Ghost" is used throughout for *heyliger Geyst*, as in TLHy's own conclusions and Trinitarian formulas; "Holy Spirit" is not used in any rendering. "Spirit" alone, as in TLHy's "Thy Spirit and grace" (Epiphany 1) or "the power of Thy Spirit" (Day of Humiliation and Prayer), stands as it is. In the revised TLHy texts each such change is recorded under "Changes from TLHy." In the new translations, the congregational forms and Part III, the earlier draft of this edition had "Holy Spirit"; all of these now read "Holy Ghost."

### 2.4 Conclusions (TLHy endings)

Dietrich usually ends with "Amen" alone, whereas TLHy closes every prayer with its full conclusion. Every collect here carries a conclusion, chosen by the rules below. **Words not in Dietrich's German are set in italics**, so that his own text can always be told apart; his "Amen" stands after them. The congregational forms carry the same endings.

- **(a) Dietrich gives no conclusion, and the Son has not been named.** In the revised TLHy texts, TLHy's own conclusion for the day is kept as TLHy prints it, with its variants (e.g., "ever one God" at Sexagesima). In the new translations: *through Thy beloved Son, Jesus Christ our Lord, who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.* Used at [13], [81], [92].
- **(b) Dietrich gives no conclusion, and the Son has already been named.** Revised TLHy: as in (a), e.g., [46]. New translations: *through the same, Thy beloved Son, Jesus Christ our Lord, who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.* Used at [6], [16], [23], [83], [89], [90], [94], [95], [96], [97], [98].
- **(c) Dietrich's last clause already contains "through Christ," "through Him" or "through Thy Son."** That phrase is moved to the end of its clause, as TLHy does at Christmas and Easter 2, and the conclusion follows it: "…through Him, *who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.*" Only the word order changes. Revised TLHy at [2], [3], [26], [34], [35], [37], [38], [40], [52], [56], [63], [64], [67], [70]; new translations at [8], [30], [79], [84], [85].
- **(d) Dietrich already closes with Christ.** His close is kept and continued, as TLHy does at Easter 1 and Trinity 8: "…through Jesus Christ, Thy Son and our Lord, *who liveth and reigneth…*". Revised TLHy at [5], [15], [27], [28], [32], [36], [42], [44], [53], [54], [61]; new translations at [29] ("for the sake of Thy dear Son and our Redeemer, Jesus Christ") and [91].
- **(e) Prayers to Christ.** *Thou who livest and reignest with the Father and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.* This is TLHy's form at Trinity 5 ([51]). Used also at [93], and at [80], whose last petition is addressed to the Son.
- **(f) The prayer to the Holy Trinity, [45].** *who livest and reignest, one God, world without end.* This is the prayer-book ending for a prayer addressed to the Holy Trinity, as in the Book of Common Prayer's collect for Trinity Sunday. It reads "one God" where TLHy's other endings read "one true God"; the prayer-book wording is kept.
- **(g) Dietrich's own Trinitarian conclusion.** Nothing is added. Where TLHy had replaced or enlarged his close, Dietrich's words are restored ([18], [68], [71]); at Pentecost ([43]) TLHy's rendering of his close is accurate and is kept. At [86]/[87] the prayer closes "…through Him live eternally with Thee and Thy Holy Ghost, and be saved."

The two appended prayers in Part III end with their own conclusions, and nothing is added to them.

### 2.5 How each entry is laid out

1. **Heading:** Stratman's number and the day in TLHy's calendar.
2. **Dietrich's heading:** the section heading in the *Summaria*, with an English rendering in brackets.
3. **German:** the prayer as printed, with its PDF page.
4. **Revised TLHy** (TLHy's collect, corrected to the German) or **New translation** (where TLHy lacks the prayer).
5. **Congregational form**, where Dietrich prays in the first person singular.
6. **Changes from TLHy** (revised texts) or **Notes** (new translations).

Stratman's modern versions are compared with the German in Appendix B.

### Dedicatory letter to Johann Neudörffer (PDF pp. 5–12)

The letter runs on without a break in the print. The paragraph divisions (¶1–¶10) are editorial and are the same in the German and the English. Each paragraph gives the German first (indented) and the translation after it. [p. 6] and the like mark where a new PDF page begins.

**Address (PDF p. 5)**

> Dem Ersamen/vnd namhafften Johann Newdörffer/deutscher Schulmeyster in Nürmberg/Meinem sonder lieben freunde.

To the honorable and renowned Johann Neudörffer, German schoolmaster in Nuremberg, my especially dear friend.

**¶1** (p. 5)

> DEr heylige Simeon/da er zu Jerusalem im Tempel/das kindlin Jesus in seinen armē hat/weyssagt er/Es werde nicht yederman diß kindlin annemen/noch für seinen heyland haben/sonder der meyste theyl/werde jm widersprechen/Vnd sein lere/so die eynige rechte seligkeyt ist/lestern/vnnd alß Ketzerey verdammen/Ob nun solches den Christen sehr werde wehe thun/soll es doch dazu dienen/das viler hertzen gedancken offenbar werden/vnnd wir die leut recht erkennen lernen/vnd durch falschen scheyn/nicht betrogen werden.

Holy Simeon, when he has the child Jesus in his arms in the Temple at Jerusalem, prophesies that not everyone will receive this child or hold Him for his Savior, but that the greater part will speak against Him, and will blaspheme His doctrine, which is the one true salvation, and condemn it as heresy. Now although this will grieve Christians sorely, it shall nevertheless serve to this end: that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed, and that we may learn to know people rightly and not be deceived by a false appearance.

**¶2** (pp. 6–7)

> [p. 6] Solche weyssagung gehet für vnnd für vnter den Christen/Bald/das liebe Euangelion sich lest hören/ist es allenthalb vol zungen/die jhm widersprechen/vnd alß Ketzerey vnd verfürische lehr es verdammen/Ja es ist an dem widersprechen nit gnug/man nimbt auch das schwert in die faust/vñ wil es mit gewalt dempffen. Werden derhalb die armen Christen mit zweyen schwerten geschlagen/Das eine durchdringet jnen jr hertz/Denn es ye einen grossen grewlichen schmertzen geberē muß/das die welt so blind ist/den gnedigen Gott im himel so lestert/dem Teuffel so willig diener/dem wort/da doch all jr seligkeyt anligt/so bitter feynd/vnnd der lügen/dem ergsten seelengifft/so vber alle massen zugethon vnnd hold ist/Das ander schwert/Nemlich/die verfolgung/mattet/verwundet/vnd würget den leyb/Solches soll mit disem kind/mit seim wort/dem heyligen Euangelio/vnd darnach mit allen Christen/für vnd für practiciert vnd geübt werden.

This prophecy comes to pass continually among Christians. As soon as the dear gospel makes itself heard, everywhere is full of tongues that speak against it and condemn it as heresy and seductive doctrine. Indeed, the speaking against is not enough; men also take the sword in hand and would stifle it by force. Poor Christians are therefore struck with two swords. The one pierces through their heart. For it must surely bring forth a great and dreadful pain that the world is so blind, so blasphemes the gracious God in heaven, is so willing a servant to the devil, so bitter an enemy to the word, on which all its salvation yet depends, and is so devoted and attached beyond all measure to the lie, the worst poison of souls. The other sword, namely persecution, wearies, wounds, and slays the body. This shall be practiced and carried out continually upon this child, upon His word, the holy gospel, and thereafter upon all Christians.

**¶3** (p. 7)

> [p. 7] Was aber für ein ergetzligkeit in yenem leben werde folgen/wissen wir auß Gottes wort lautter vnd gewiß/deñ es wirt ein vberauß herrlich/frölich/vnd wonreychs wort sein/wenn der Son Gottes sagen wirt: Kombt her jr gesegneten meines Vatters/ererbet das Reych/das euch bereytet ist/von anbegin der welt. Die hunde aber/vnnd die Zauberer(wie inn der Offenbarung am 22. stehet) Vnd die Hurer/vnd die Todschleger/vnd die Abgöttischen/vnd alle die lieb haben vnd thun die lügen/werden hinunder müssen inn das hellische fewer/vnnd ewige flammen/Da wirt sich alß denn der rechte wechssel finden.

But what delight will follow in the life beyond, we know clearly and certainly from God's word. For it will be an exceedingly glorious, joyful, and blissful word when the Son of God shall say: "Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world." But the dogs, and the sorcerers (as it stands in Revelation 22), and the whoremongers, and the murderers, and the idolaters, and all who love and make a lie, will have to go down into the fire of hell and the eternal flames. There, then, the right exchange will be found.

**¶4** (pp. 7–8)

> Hie aber auff erden/sagt Simeon/sollen die Feynde des worts offenbar werden/Das alle Christen jhnen in das hertz hinein sehen/vnd gründlich sie vrteylen können/wer sie sind/wie sie leben vnd sterben/vnd was für ein vrtheyl sie für Gott in ewigkeyt tragen müssen/Solchs ist nicht ein geringes/damit die Christen inn der verfolgung [p. 8] sich mögē trösten.

But here on earth, says Simeon, the enemies of the word shall be revealed, so that all Christians can see into their heart and judge them thoroughly: who they are, how they live and die, and what judgment they must bear before God in eternity. That is no small thing, with which Christians may comfort themselves in persecution.

**¶5** (p. 8)

> Wir haben in deutschen land nun ein lange zeyt das widersprechen/starck hören vnd dulden müssen/Yetzundt kombt das schwert mit gewalt dazu/das der Antichrist zu Rom/mit seim Anhang vnsere kirchen gar will tilgen/vnd verwüsten/vnd durch sein Antichristisch Conciliabel zu Trient/alle abgötterey vnd lügen wider anrichten. Solches fürnemen/schmucken sie zierlich vnnd fein/vnd ist gewiß/wer jhren worten will glauben/der ist schon betrogen. Denn anderst hörest nicht von jnen/denn das sie die strittigen Religion/vergleychen/eynigkeyt anrichten/vñ die Scheflein in den rechten stal bringen wöllen.

We in the German land have now for a long time had to hear and endure the speaking against in full force. Now the sword comes with force besides, in that the Antichrist at Rome, with his adherents, would utterly destroy and lay waste our churches, and through his antichristian conciliabulum at Trent set up again all idolatry and lies. This undertaking they adorn elegantly and finely, and it is certain that whoever will believe their words is already deceived. For you hear nothing else from them than that they would settle the disputed religion, establish unity, and bring the little sheep into the right fold.

**¶6** (pp. 8–9)

> Vnd kan wol sein/das jr vil/so zu solchem blutdürstigen fürnemen helffen/jres wandels vnd lebens halb from̃ sind/Aber was saget Simeon/bald du das widersprechen hörest/das sie des Herrn Christi wort nicht wöllen leyden/noch war lassen sein/so frag nicht weyter/jr hertz stehet dir so glat vnd gar offen/alß hettest [p. 9] du es in deiner hand/Kanst derhalb vrtheylen/das es eytel Heucheley mit jnen ist/das der Teuffel jhnen im hertzen sitzt/sie treybet vnd füret/Vnd in summa/das sie gar des Teuffels sind/wie sie stehen vnd gehen/vnangesehen jres ampts/stands/wandels/zucht/vñ was sonst mehr für tugend an jnen mögen gerhümet werden. Denn das Euangelion verfolgen/den Son Gottes nicht allein für vnsern erlöser halten/Andere mittel der seligkeyt den leuten fürhalten/vnd suchen/Abgötterey verteydigen ꝛc. solches ist ein sünde/die nicht allein hie auff erden mit allerley plagen/sonder in yener welt/mit dem hellischen ewigen fewer/muß gestraffet werden.

And it may well be that many of those who help in such a bloodthirsty undertaking are upright as regards their conduct and life. But what does Simeon say? As soon as you hear the speaking against, that they will not suffer the word of the Lord Christ nor let it be true, then ask no further: their heart stands as plainly and wide open to you as if you had it in your hand. You can therefore judge that it is sheer hypocrisy with them, that the devil sits in their heart, drives and leads them, and, in sum, that they are wholly the devil's, as they stand and go, without regard to their office, station, conduct, discipline, and whatever other virtue may be praised in them. For to persecute the gospel, not to hold the Son of God alone for our Redeemer, to set other means of salvation before people and to seek them, to defend idolatry, etc.: this is a sin which must be punished not only here on earth with all manner of plagues, but in the world beyond with the eternal fire of hell.

**¶7** (pp. 9–10)

> Da sehen wir/die wir sonst des Creutzes/vnd der verfolgung halben/kleinmütig vnnd verzagt sind/was für einen grossen treffenlichen vortheyl/wir für der welt haben/ob gleich die selb mit gewalt/ehren/gut/vnd anders vns vberlegē ist. Sollen derhalb dem genedigen Gott im Hymel/ [p. 10] nicht vndanckbar für solche gnade sein/vnd sonderlich weyl die welt/mit dem widersprechen nicht will ablassen/sonder noch das schwert dazu in die faust nimbt/sollen wir beden/dem Teuffel vnnd der welt zu trotz/Vnnd darnach dem Son Gottes zu ehren/vnnd vnseren nachkommen zu nutz/mit dem bekentniß in alle weg/so vns müglich/getrewlich vnd fleyssig anhalten/vnd vnsere rechte Christenliche lehr/auff alle weyse zu pflantzen/vnd erweytern versuchen.

Here we, who are otherwise fainthearted and despondent on account of the cross and of persecution, see what a great and excellent advantage we have over the world, although it is superior to us in power, honors, wealth, and other things. We should therefore not be unthankful to the gracious God in heaven for such grace. And especially, since the world will not leave off the speaking against, but takes the sword in hand besides, we should, in defiance of both the devil and the world, and further to the honor of the Son of God and for the good of our descendants, persevere faithfully and diligently in the confession in every way possible to us, and try in every manner to plant and extend our true Christian doctrine.

**¶8** (pp. 10–11)

> Sonderlich aber sollen wir auff das junge volck sehen/das es inn zeyt zu Gottes wort gehalten/vnnd trewlich damit vnterrichtet werde. Solche vrsach hat mich bewegt/dise kurtze Euangelien Summaria/so ich für meine kindlein zuschreyben newlich angefangen/yetzt diser zeyt/da ich schwachheyt halb/mein ampt in der Kirch/nicht hab selb verwalten können/gar zuuerfertigen/vnnd der jugend zu gut inn druck zu geben/auff das/wer lust dazu hat/meiner arbeyt [p. 11] möchte geniessen/ob ich gleych stetter kranckheyt halb/das predigampt hab müssen anstehen lassen.

But especially we should look to the young people, that they be kept to God's word early and faithfully instructed in it. This reason has moved me, now at this time when on account of weakness I have not been able to administer my office in the church myself, to finish completely these short summaries of the Gospels, which I recently began to write for my little children, and to give them to the press for the good of the youth, so that whoever has a desire for it might have the benefit of my labor, although on account of continual illness I have had to let the office of preaching rest.

**¶9** (p. 11)

> Jch hab aber günstiger vnnd lieber Newdörffer/euch vnd ewer schule/damit verehren wöllen/weyl jr/alß ein Christlicher trewer Schulmeyster/ewr gesindlin sonderlich fleyssig zu Gottes wort haltet/das ich nicht allein zu solchem gutem werck euch hülffe/sonder durch ewr Exempel/auch andere deudsche vnnd lateynische Schulmeyster/reytzete/ewrem Exempel nach/hie vnd an andern orten/die jugend fleyssig zu Gottes wort zu halten. Denn da müssen wir anheben/so wir anders Christliche lehr erhalten/vnnd Gottes namen heyligen/Ja auch fromme/gefölgige/züchtige/vnd glückselige Kinder haben wöllen.

But, kind and dear Neudörffer, I have wished to honor you and your school with it, because you, as a Christian and faithful schoolmaster, keep your little household especially diligently to God's word; so that I might not only help you in such a good work, but through your example might also stir up other German and Latin schoolmasters, after your example, here and in other places, to keep the youth diligently to God's word. For there we must begin, if indeed we would preserve Christian doctrine and hallow God's name, yes, and also have godly, obedient, well-disciplined, and happy children.

**¶10** (pp. 11–12)

> Bey den Papisten soll diß klein Büchlein/so vnansehenlich es ist/sein frucht auch reychlich mit bringen/das es zum ewigen zeuckniß/sampt andern der vnsern schrifften offentlich da stehen sol/das sie vñ yederman sehen/was doch [p. 12] mangels an vnser lehr sey/die sie bißher mit lestern so hart angefochten/vnd yetzundt mit dem schwert gar zu tilgen sich vnterfangen haben. Aber sie sollens nicht enden/Dise lehr/soll vnd muß bleyben/Sie aber/wenn sie es nit wöllen leyden/müssen drüber zu grund gehen/vñ soll sie kein macht noch herrligkeyt helffen. Der Allmechtige/wölle euch/vnd alle fromme Christen/sonderlich zu diser kömmerlichen zeyt/gnedig erhalten vnd behüten/Amen.

Among the papists too this little book, unimpressive as it is, shall bring its fruit abundantly, in that it shall stand publicly, together with other writings of ours, as an everlasting testimony, so that they and everyone may see what fault, after all, there is in our doctrine, which they have hitherto assailed so hard with blasphemy and have now undertaken to destroy utterly with the sword. But they shall not accomplish it. This doctrine shall and must remain. But they, if they will not suffer it, must perish over it, and no power or glory shall help them. May the Almighty graciously preserve and protect you and all godly Christians, especially in this sorrowful time. Amen.

**Date and subscription (p. 12)**

> Geschriben den andern September/in meiner studier vnd siech stube. Anno 1546.
>
> E. W. Vitus Dietrich.

Written on the second of September, in my study and sickroom, in the year 1546.

Your willing [servant], Vitus Dietrich.

**Notes**
- **Neudörffer (Address).** Johann Neudörffer the Elder (1497–1563) was Nuremberg's celebrated writing and arithmetic master. *deutscher Schulmeyster* means the master of a German school, one that taught in German rather than in Latin; Dietrich draws the same distinction in ¶9 ("German and Latin schoolmasters"). *sonder lieben* is "especially dear."
- **Simeon (¶1, ¶4).** The letter is built on Luke 2:34–35. *widersprechen* ("speak against") is the verb of Luke 2:34 in Luther's Bible ("ein Zeichen, dem widersprochen wird"; King James: "a sign which shall be spoken against"), and the noun *das widersprechen* is rendered "the speaking against" throughout. "That the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed" is Luke 2:35 in King James wording, which Dietrich's German follows closely.
- **The two swords (¶2).** The first sword "pierces through" the heart, applying to all Christians Simeon's word to Mary, "a sword shall pierce through thy own soul also" (Luke 2:35). The second is persecution.
- **"So willing a servant to the devil" (¶2).** The print reads *diener* ("servant"), checked at 400 dpi. *dienet* ("serves") would also make sense, but the noun matches the parallel *feynd* ("enemy") in the next clause.
- **Scripture in ¶3.** The words of the Son of God are Matthew 25:34. The list of the condemned is Revelation 22:15, in King James wording; the rest of that sentence, that they "must go down into the fire of hell," is Dietrich's own (compare Revelation 21:8).
- **"The right exchange" (¶3).** *wechssel* means exchange or reversal: in the life beyond, present fortunes are turned around.
- **Trent (¶5).** The Council of Trent had opened in December 1545. *Conciliabel* (Latin *conciliabulum*) is the term for an illegitimate council; Dietrich denies Trent the name of a council. *vergleychen* ("settle") is the vocabulary of the religious colloquies that sought to reconcile the two parties; Dietrich had himself taken part in the one held at Regensburg in 1546.
- **The sword "now" (¶5, ¶7, ¶10).** The letter is dated 2 September 1546, during the Schmalkaldic War, which had broken out that summer. This is the sword that, Dietrich says, has now been added to the speaking against.
- **"As they stand and go" (¶6).** An idiom for "in all that they are and do."
- **Dietrich's illness (¶8, subscription).** He says he can neither administer his office nor preach, because of continual illness, and dates the letter from his "study and sickroom." He died at Nuremberg on 25 March 1549.
- **"Honor you with it" (¶9).** *verehren* here has its older sense of honoring someone with a gift, that is, presenting the book to him.
- **"Your little household" (¶9).** *gesindlin* is the diminutive of *Gesinde*, a household; here it evidently means Neudörffer's pupils.
- **Subscription.** *E. W.* is taken as the usual abbreviation for *Ewer Williger*, "your willing [servant]."
- **Two-word forms (¶7).** *ob gleich* ("although") and *die selb* ("the same") are printed as two words and are kept so.
