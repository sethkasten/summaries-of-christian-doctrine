## PART III. APPENDED PRAYERS (PDF pp. 299–303)

After Dietrich's address to the reader (pp. 297–298, signed "Veit Dietrich."), the book prints two further prayers before the colophon. Neither is a Gospel collect, and neither appears in Stratman or TLHy. They are given here in the same form as the collects. Each ends with its own conclusion, so no TLHy ending is added.

### Appended prayer 1. For children in perilous times (pp. 299–300)

**Heading (PDF p. 299):** *Wie die Eltern jhre kindlin zu disen fehrlichen zeyten/vmb erhaltung des wort Gotes/wider die feinde des heyligen Euangelij/sollen lernen betten.* [How parents should teach their little children to pray, in these perilous times, for the preservation of God's word against the enemies of the holy gospel.]

**German (PDF pp. 299–300)**

> HErr Got himlischer Vater/wir dancken dir/dz du vns das selige liecht deines wortes so gnediglich angezündet/vñ biß her hast lassen leuchten/vnd bitten dich/Du wöllest ja zu diser zeyt ob solchem liecht gnediglich halten/vnd den Sathan vnd der argen welt nit gestatten/das sie es außleschen. Laß dich vnser erbarmē lieber Vater/vber welche solcher jam̃er sonderlich würde außgehen. Wir sind noch jung vnd vnerzogen/vnd bedürffen für vnd für/das wir in deiner forcht/vnd in deim wort von vnsern eltern vnterrichtet werden/vnnd dich von tag zu tage/ye lenger ye baß erkennē lernen/So gehn aber die feinde deines worts da mit vmb/das sie vns in abgötterey vnd finsternuß füren/vñ das wort vns gar entziehen wöllen/Solchem jammer lieber Vater/wehre du vmb deines namens willen. Du sprichst/du wöllest dein lob zu zurichtē auß dem munde der vnmündigen vñ seugling/Vmb solche gnad bitten wir dich yetz/lieber vater/gibe deiner Kirchen fride/vnnd wehre allen feinden deines worts/die jetzund vns bedrangen/auff das wir vnd vnsere brüderlin vñ schwesterlin/so teglich hernach wachsen/solches gnedigts [sic, for gnedigs] liecht auch haben/vnnd dich mit vnserm gebet frü vnd abens loben/anruffen/vnnd bekennen/Der du vnser einiger Gott vnd ewiger trost bist mit deinem Son vnsern Herrn Christo Jesu/vnd dem heiligen Geist/Amen.

**Translation**

> Lord God, heavenly Father, we thank Thee that Thou hast so graciously kindled for us the blessed light of Thy word, and hast suffered it to shine until now; and we beseech Thee, do Thou at this time graciously uphold this light, and suffer not Satan and the wicked world to quench it. Have mercy upon us, dear Father, upon whom such misery would especially fall. We are yet young and untrained, and have continual need to be taught by our parents in Thy fear and in Thy word, and to learn to know Thee better and better from day to day. But the enemies of Thy word go about to lead us into idolatry and darkness, and would take the word from us altogether. Such misery, dear Father, do Thou prevent for Thy name's sake. Thou sayest that Thou wilt perfect Thy praise out of the mouth of babes and sucklings: for such grace we now beseech Thee, dear Father, give peace to Thy Church, and restrain all the enemies of Thy word who now oppress us; that we and our little brothers and sisters, who are daily growing up after us, may also have this gracious light, and with our prayer morning and evening may praise, call upon, and confess Thee, who with Thy Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost art our only God and everlasting comfort. Amen.

**Notes**
- **Voice.** The heading frames this as a prayer that parents are to teach their children. The prayer itself speaks in the children's voice ("We are yet young and untrained…").
- **Scripture.** *dein lob zu zurichten auß dem munde der vnmündigen vñ seugling* follows Matthew 21:16 (cf. Psalm 8:2), rendered in King James wording ("perfected praise").
- **Rendering choices.**
  - *ob solchem liecht … halten* means to uphold or keep hold over this light.
  - *da mit vmb gehen* is "go about to" (cf. John 7:19, King James).
  - *wehren* (with dative) means to restrain or prevent.
  - *abens* = *abends* (evening).
- **Name.** *Christo Jesu* is given as "Jesus Christ," as in the collects (§2.2).

**Heading (PDF p. 301):** *Ein gebet D. Johannis Bugenhagen/Pomerani/Pfarhern zu Wittemberg.* [A prayer of Dr. Johannes Bugenhagen, Pomeranus, Pastor at Wittenberg.]

**German (PDF pp. 301–303)**

> WArhafftiger/Ewiger/Allmechtiger Gott/Vatter vnsers Heylandts Jhesu Christi/schöpffer himmels vnd der Erden/sampt deinem ewigen Son/vñ heyligen Geyst/Wir bekennen/das wir leider vil sünde haben/vnd nicht allein vnwissentlich/sonder auch wissentlich mit grosser vndanckbarkeit gesündiget/vnnd haben deinen zorn vnd straff wol verdienet/vnd haben ein hertzlich mißfallen an vnsern sünden.
>
> Wir bittē aber von hertzen/du wöllest vns alle vnsere sünde/vmb deines lieben Sons willen Jesu Christi/den du zum versöner gesetzt hast/gnediglich vergeben/vnd mit deinem heiligē Geist vnsere hertzen regiern/das wir in rechtē glauben/rechter anrüffung/rechtem gehorsam für dir allezeit lebē.
>
> Vñ dieweil du sihest die grosse not vnser Herrschafft/vnser aller/man/weib vnd Kinder/vnd wir one deine hülff keinē schutz habē/Erkennest auch aller Menschen hertzen/vnd sihest das vnsere Feinde fürnemlich suchen vertilgūg [type damaged; = vertilgung] rechter lere/vnd auffrichtung vnd bestettigung jrer schendlichen Abgötterey vnd vnzucht/So bitten wir dich von hertzen/durch vnsern Heilandt Jesum Christum/du wöllest vmb deiner Ehre willen/vnser Herrschafft/vnser Kirchen/vns/vnser kinder vñ heußlin/gnediglich jetzund vñ allezeit schützen vnnd bewaren/wie du dein volck Jsrael im roten Meer erhalten vnd geschützet hast/vnnd wöllest der feinde rath vnd macht zerstören/vnd die mördische frembde Nation/jhre vnzucht vñ grausamkeit nicht an vnsern Weib vnd Kindern üben lassen.
>
> Du wöllest auch vmb deiner Ehre willen/fürthin deine lere vnd Christliche [final e damaged] selige regiment/in disen Landen gnediglich erhalten/vñ vnsere Herrschafft mit deinem heyligen Geyst/zu Christlicher seliger regierung neigen/das sie vnd die Vnterthan/in rechter erkentnus Jesu Christi/in rechter anrüffung vñ gehorsam gegen dir/vnd in frieden leben vnd bleiben/vnd dich ewiglich preisen vnd dir dancken. Du hast gesprochen/Rüff mich an in der not/so wil ich dich erhören/das du mich preysen solt/Solche deine wort sind one zweiffel war/Darumb bit ich dich/du wöllest vns in diser grossen not auch erhören/vmb deines Sons willen Jesu Christi/vnnd wöllest vns gnediglich bewaren/AMEN.

**Translation**

> True, eternal, and almighty God, Father of our Savior Jesus Christ, Creator of heaven and earth, together with Thine eternal Son and Holy Ghost: We confess that, alas, we have much sin, and have sinned not only unwittingly but also wittingly, with great ingratitude, and have well deserved Thy wrath and punishment; and we have a hearty displeasure at our sins.
>
> But we pray from our hearts that Thou wouldest graciously forgive us all our sins for the sake of Thy dear Son, Jesus Christ, whom Thou hast appointed to be the Reconciler, and govern our hearts with Thy Holy Ghost, that we may live before Thee at all times in true faith, true invocation, and true obedience.
>
> And since Thou seest the great need of our rulers and of us all, men, women, and children, and that without Thy help we have no protection; since Thou knowest also the hearts of all men, and seest that our enemies chiefly seek the destruction of true doctrine and the setting up and establishing of their shameful idolatry and lewdness: we therefore beseech Thee from our hearts, through our Savior Jesus Christ, that for Thine honor's sake Thou wouldest graciously protect and keep, now and at all times, our rulers, our church, ourselves, our children, and our households, as Thou didst preserve and protect Thy people Israel in the Red Sea; and that Thou wouldest destroy the counsel and might of the enemies, and not suffer the murderous foreign nation to practice its lewdness and cruelty upon our wives and children.
>
> Do Thou also, for Thine honor's sake, henceforth graciously preserve Thy doctrine and Christian and blessed government in these lands, and by Thy Holy Ghost incline our rulers to Christian and blessed rule, that they and the subjects may live and abide in the true knowledge of Jesus Christ, in true invocation of Thee and obedience toward Thee, and in peace, and may praise Thee and give thanks unto Thee forever. Thou hast said: "Call upon Me in trouble, and I will hear thee, that thou shalt glorify Me." Such words of Thine are without doubt true. Therefore I beseech Thee, hear us also in this great need, for the sake of Thy Son, Jesus Christ, and graciously preserve us. Amen.

**Notes**
- **Psalm 50:15.** Bugenhagen quotes the verse with *erhören* ("hear"), where Luther's Bible reads *erretten* ("deliver"). The translation follows Bugenhagen.
- **Rendering choices.**
  - *versöner* (*Versöhner*) is "Reconciler."
  - *Herrschafft* is the ruling authority, here "our rulers."
  - *anrüffung* is invocation, the calling upon God.
  - *vnzucht* is unchastity or lewdness.
  - *heußlin* are little houses, that is, households.
- **Person.** The prayer speaks as "we" throughout but closes *Darumb bit ich dich* ("Therefore I beseech Thee"); this is kept as printed.
- **Paragraphs.** The paragraph breaks follow the print.
- **Setting (an inference).** The prayer asks protection for rulers, church and households against enemies bent on rooting out true doctrine and setting up idolatry, and against a "murderous foreign nation." In a book printed at Nuremberg in 1548, whose dedication (2 September 1546) speaks of the sword now added to the speaking against the gospel (front matter, ¶5), this points to the Schmalkaldic War of 1546–47, and the unnamed "foreign nation" is presumably the emperor's foreign soldiery. Bugenhagen addressed the Saxon clergy on that war in 1546 (*Von der jetzigen Kriegsrüstung*). Whether this prayer comes from that writing has not been checked.

## Appendix B. Stratman compared

Paul C. Stratman's *The Collects of Veit Dietrich* gives every collect in modern English. It was used throughout as a check on accuracy, never as a source of wording.

**Where TLHy has the collect.** Stratman's text is a modernization of TLHy. Thee/Thou becomes you, relative clauses become statements, "We beseech Thee" is usually dropped before the imperative, and long sentences are divided. The conclusion becomes "who lives and reigns … now and forever," and the third Person is named in the modern form throughout. "Quicken" becomes "awaken," "carnal security" becomes "worldly security," "covetousness" becomes "greed," "Thy holy word" becomes "your precious Word," and "became man" becomes "became fully human." "The Jews" becomes "your people long ago," and "the darkness of Papacy" becomes "the darkness of error." Because these texts follow TLHy, they share TLHy's departures from the German; those departures are listed under "Changes from TLHy" in each entry and are not repeated here.

**Where TLHy lacks the collect.** Stratman's texts are his own fresh translations in the same modern idiom. Each was checked clause by clause against the German. Most are sound paraphrases; his substantive departures are listed below, and the German has been followed in every case. The most consequential are these:
- [6]: *verfolgen* ("persecute") is taken as "do not follow."
- [8]: the subject and sense of the central clauses are reversed.
- [23]: the blind man's sight is transferred to "us."
- [29]: a causal clause is turned into a petition.
- [32] and [84]: *dauon ablassen* ("forsake them") is taken as "receive forgiveness."
- [83]: the petition against the tyrants is recast as a prayer for rulers' goodwill.
- [96]: *sich … versehen* ("look to [God] for") is taken as "share with others."
- [97]: the central image, the angels' hedge, is replaced.

**[10].** Stratman prints this prayer twice in modern dress, as [10] (Sunday after New Year, from the *Summaria*) and [24] (Quinquagesima, from TLHy), following TLHy's wording both times.

**[6] First Sunday of Christmas (Sunday after Christmas)**

- He reads *die so dein wort verfolgen* as "those who do not follow your Word." *verfolgen* means "persecute."
- He drops "rise again in Him" and "stumble at Him."
- He gives "rightly confess" for *recht erkennen* ("rightly know").
- He merges two separate clauses into "not be blinded by the false security of the world or by sufferings and afflictions."

**[8] The Circumcision / New Year's Day**

- He makes the Son the subject ("your Son put himself under the law").
- He renders the comfort clause "comfort despite such disobedience." The German speaks of Christ's obedience as our comfort.
- He gives "remain your obedient children" where the German has *anfahen*, "begin to be" obedient children.

**[13] First Sunday after Epiphany**

- "reached out to me with the blessing of baptism" is a paraphrase.
- He omits "and will."
- He gives "not forget" for *versaume* ("neglect").
- "May he dwell in my heart" is not in the German.
- He omits the closing "Amen," which the German has.

**[16] Third Sunday after Epiphany**

- He has "We have your Word"; the German says *His* word.
- He omits "with a word."
- He gives "remain in the faith until our last hour" for "at our last end abide firmly thereby."

**[23] Quinquagesima**

- He transfers the light from the blind man to "us" ("have let us poor sinners see your light").
- He omits "by Thy dear word."
- He makes Christ the object of knowledge; the German has "learn to know *Thee* through Christ."
- He gives "was counted guilty of our sins" for *für vnsere sünde bezalet* ("paid for our sins").
- He drops "sin" from "devil, sin, and death" and adds "finally be delivered in death."

**[29] Fifth Sunday in Lent (Judica)**

- He gives "remains in my word" for *helt* ("keeps").
- He turns the causal clause into a petition ("that your Son's Word may come to us and that we may hear it").
- He gives "never see eternal death" for *den todt in ewigkeyt nicht sehen*.

**[30] Palm Sunday**

**[32] Maundy Thursday**

- He gives "break us or bring us to shame" for "break in and do harm."
- He rearranges the final clause and omits "Amen."

**[98] Sts. Simon and Jude, Apostles (October 28)**

- He gives "despise the world and the devil's wrath" for "the wrath of the world and of the devil." Otherwise his text is faithful.

   - Trinity, "Matth. 4." for Matthew 3 (p. 136; [45]).
   - Third Sunday after Trinity, "Luc. 19." for Luke 15 (p. 149; [49]).
   - St. Matthias, "Matthei am 9." for Matthew 11 (p. 247; [86]).
   - On the First Sunday in Lent the reference is partly blotted, "Ma[tth]. 4." (p. 76; [25]).
2. **Headings without a Sunday or a reference.** The Septuagesima section is headed only by its parable, with no Sunday named (p. 67; [21]). The Monday after Pentecost has no Scripture reference (p. 132; [44]). Pentecost cites Joel 2 rather than its Gospel (p. 129; [43]).
3. **Duplicated prayer.** The St. Matthias prayer (pp. 250–251) is the Annunciation prayer (pp. 254–255), with a doubled "Amen/Amen." See [86].
4. **Doubled Amen.** The Third Sunday after Easter also ends "Amen/AMEN." (p. 115; [38]).
5. **Typesetting slips.**
   - p. 75: "völlest" for "wöllest" ([23]).
   - p. 80: "vom dem" for "von dem" in the heading ([26]).
   - p. 95: ")" for a virgule ([30]).
   - p. 152: "ANEN" for "AMEN" ([49]).
   - p. 198: "Gerst" for "Geyst" ([63]).
   - p. 216: "gekommen" for "genommen" ([68]).
   - p. 296: "inn sere" for "inn vnsere" ([98]).
   - The catchwords disagree with the following page at p. 262 ("geschicket" vs. "schicket") and p. 275 ("reichlich" vs. "reychlich").
6. **Order of sections.** St. Stephen sits in the temporale after Christmas. Mary Magdalene (pp. 267–271) is printed before the Visitation (pp. 272–276). The Magdalene heading states that the feast is kept on the Seventh Sunday after Trinity.
7. **Two Trinity sections.** There are two Trinity sections, each with its own prayer: the Baptism text, Matthew 3 ([45]), and the Nicodemus text, John 3 ([46], the one TLHy uses).
8. **Epiphany 2 (Cana, pp. 54–57).** The prayer is headed *Für die/so im Ehestand sind* [For those who are in the estate of marriage], is in the first person singular (with a shift to "wir" partway through), and is followed by a rubric for the unmarried, given under [15]. No further Dietrich prayer is missing for this Sunday.
9. **End matter.** The book closes with Dietrich's address to the reader (pp. 297–298), the two appended prayers (pp. 299–303; Part III), and the colophon (p. 304). The colophon carries a woodcut of the Transfiguration with the banderole DOMINE BONVM EST NOS HIC ESSE (Matthew 17:4), the line *Psalm. LXXXIX. Wol dem Volck das jauchzen kan.* [Psalm 89: Blessed is the people that can rejoice] (Psalm 89:16 in Luther's numbering), and the printers' names.
10. **Dates.** The dedication is dated 2 September 1546 (p. 12), two years before this printing (1548). Stratman gives 1546 as the year the work was first published. If so, this is a later printing, and an earlier one would be the natural place to check the Matthias duplication (item 3) and the misprints above.
