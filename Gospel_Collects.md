# The Gospel Collects of Veit Dietrich (1548)

**The German text of the *Summaria Christlicher Lehr*, with the collects of *The Lutheran Hymnary* (1913) revised against it, and the remaining collects newly translated in the same idiom**

Every Gospel collect in Dietrich's *Summaria* is given here in its original German, followed by its English. Where *The Lutheran Hymnary* (TLHy) has the prayer, its English has been checked clause by clause against the German, corrected where it departs from it, and printed as **Revised TLHy**, with every change listed beneath it. Where TLHy lacks the prayer, a **New translation** in TLHy's idiom is given instead. The index in §3 lists every collect in church-year order and links to it.

## 1. Source and scope

**Source.** Veit Dietrich, *Summaria Christlicher lehr/für das junge volck/Was auß eim yeden Sontags Euāgelio zu mercken sey/Sampt angehenckten Gebeten. durch Vitum Dietrich. M.D.XLVIII.* [Summaries of Christian Doctrine for the Young People: What Is to Be Noted from Each Sunday's Gospel, Together with Appended Prayers. By Vitus Dietrich. 1548.] Google Books digitization, 306 PDF pages. The book has signatures but no page numbers, so every page reference below is to the **PDF page** of the scan (PDF p. 3 = title page). The colophon (PDF p. 304) reads *Gedruckt zu Nürmberg/durch Johañ vom Berg/vnd Vlrich Newber.* [Printed at Nuremberg by Johann vom Berg and Ulrich Neuber.]

PDF pp. 1–2 are Google's digitization notices. The book opens with the title page (PDF p. 3; its verso, p. 4, is blank) and Dietrich's dedicatory letter to Johann Neudörffer, dated 2 September 1546 (pp. 5–12). The temporale runs PDF pp. 13–228 (First Sunday in Advent through the Twenty-fifth Sunday after Trinity, with St. Stephen's Day placed after Christmas). The sanctorale, headed *Summaria Christlicher lehr auß den Euangelien der fürnembsten Fest durchs Jar* [Summaries of Christian doctrine from the Gospels of the principal feasts through the year], runs pp. 229–296 (St. Andrew through Sts. Simon and Jude). After the sanctorale come Dietrich's address *Dem Christlichen Leser* [To the Christian Reader] (pp. 297–298), two appended prayers (pp. 299–303) and the colophon (p. 304).

**Scope.** This edition gives every prayer that Stratman marks (S), meaning it comes from the *Summaria*: 80 numbered collects. Because [86] and [87] are the same prayer printed twice, there are 79 distinct texts. Bracketed numbers are Stratman's throughout.

- **54 collects are in TLHy.** Each is printed as *Revised TLHy*, with its changes listed. They include [10], which TLHy prints at Quinquagesima and which is restored here to the Sunday after New Year, and Maundy Thursday, where TLHy's collect ([33]) proves to be a free adaptation of Dietrich's [32].
- **26 collects are not in TLHy.** Each is given in a *New translation* in TLHy's idiom. Among them is Dietrich's own Quinquagesima collect [23].
- **TLHy's other collects**, which do not come from Dietrich, are given in Appendix A, so that every TLHy day can be found. With them, for reference, are TLHy's own texts of [24] and [33] (Dietrich's [10] and [32]) and the harvest collect [77], so that Appendix A holds every collect Stratman marks (H).
- **The four collects Stratman marks (ELH)**, [73], [75], [76] and [78], are given in Appendix B with their origins and a version in TLHy's idiom. Two of them, [75] and [78], prove to be in TLHy after all, in its second series of collects.
- The front matter (title page and dedicatory letter) precedes Part I, and Part III gives the two prayers appended at the end of the book.

## 2. Conventions

### 2.1 Transcription

- **Semi-diplomatic.** Original spelling, capitalization and punctuation are kept, including the virgule (/). Abbreviations stay as printed: vñ = vnd, dz = das, and a bar or tilde over a letter stands for a following m or n (lebē = leben, stim̃ = stimm). Long ſ and r rotunda (ꝛ) are normalized to s and r. Superscript-e vowels are given as ä, ö, ü. Words broken at a line end are joined without comment.
- **Decorated initials.** The initial is printed as part of the first word (HErr, HERR, ALmechtiger, O Jesu).
- **j-forms.** The printer sets the pronoun forms *jn, jm, jhn, jren* with a descending j, distinct from the preposition *in*. This was checked at 400 dpi and is followed here.
- **Misprints and damage.** Misprints are kept and marked [sic], with the intended reading. Damaged type is noted where it occurs.
- **Front matter.** The title page is given line by line. In the dedicatory letter, [p. 6] and the like mark where a new PDF page begins; when a page ends inside a divided word, the marker follows that word. Catchwords and signatures are omitted, and ꝛc., the sign for *et cetera*, is kept as printed.
- **Checking.** Every transcription was read from the page images at 200 dpi, and doubtful points were checked at 400 dpi. The scan's OCR layer was used only to find pages and, afterwards, to cross-check that no words or lines had been skipped.

### 2.2 TLHy's idiom, and how it has been revised

Every TLHy collect that comes from the *Summaria* was set beside Dietrich's German and compared clause by clause. The comparison shows how TLHy worked:

1. **Register and syntax.** TLHy uses Thee/Thou/Thy/Thine, -est/-eth verbs and capitalized divine pronouns. It shapes each prayer as a collect: invocation, a relative clause ("who hast…"), a colon, then the petition ("We beseech Thee…"). Scripture echoes are given in King James wording.
2. **Plural for singular.** TLHy recasts Dietrich's first-person-singular prayers in the plural (Epiphany 2; Trinity 11, 12, 19 and 20).
3. **Conclusions.** TLHy adds its full conclusion to every prayer, whether Dietrich ends with no conclusion at all, with a short "durch Christum…" (e.g., Easter 1, Trinity 8), or with a Trinitarian close of his own (Epiphany 4; Trinity 22 and 25).
4. **Free paraphrase.** TLHy often paraphrases. It drops clauses (Epiphany 4's "to hear our prayer in all temptation and need"; Trinity 13's "and sooner forsake all things"), adds others (Maundy Thursday's "remember Thine agony and death"; Trinity 8's prayer to "send us now and ever pious pastors"), and at times reverses the sense (Trinity 18 blames "the weakness of our flesh and blood," where Dietrich says that flesh and blood are too *strong* in us). At Maundy Thursday it readdresses Dietrich's prayer to Christ.

**How TLHy is revised here.**

- *Kept:* TLHy's register, syntax, collect shape and "We beseech Thee"; its settled equivalents wherever they render the German without loss (table below); King James wording wherever Dietrich's German follows the same verse ("overcharged with surfeiting and drunkenness, and cares of this life," Advent 2; "an honest and good heart," Sexagesima; "besides women and children," Trinity 7); and TLHy's conclusions (§2.4).
- *Corrected:* omissions are restored; additions are removed; changed sense is given literally; Dietrich's address, person and number are restored, and for the singular prayers a plural "congregational form" is supplied, as TLHy's practice requires; *dein liebes wort* is "Thy dear word," not "Thy holy word"; and "Holy Ghost" replaces "Holy Spirit" (§2.3). Slips in the Orthodox Lutheran Ministerium's web text of TLHy, such as "Holy Gaost" or a closing "Amen,", are corrected and noted.
- *Recorded:* every change is listed under **Changes from TLHy**, in the form "TLHy wording" → "revised wording," followed by the German that requires it.

The new translations follow the same rules, so that the two kinds of text can stand side by side. The prose of the front matter is translated in formal modern English instead, with Scripture still in King James wording.

### 2.3 "Holy Ghost"

"Holy Ghost" is used throughout for *heyliger Geyst*, as in TLHy's own conclusions and Trinitarian formulas; "Holy Spirit" is not used in any rendering. "Spirit" alone, as in TLHy's "Thy Spirit and grace" (Epiphany 1) or "the power of Thy Spirit" (Day of Humiliation and Prayer), stands as it is. In the revised TLHy texts each such change is recorded under "Changes from TLHy." In the new translations, the congregational forms and Part III, the earlier draft of this edition had "Holy Spirit"; all of these now read "Holy Ghost."

### 2.4 Conclusions (TLHy endings)

Dietrich usually ends with "Amen" alone, whereas TLHy closes every prayer with its full conclusion. Every collect here carries a conclusion, chosen by the rules below. **Words not in Dietrich's German are set in italics**, so that his own text can always be told apart; his "Amen" stands after them. The congregational forms carry the same endings.

- **(a) Dietrich gives no conclusion, and the Son has not been named.** In the revised TLHy texts, TLHy's own conclusion for the day is kept as TLHy prints it, with its variants (e.g., "ever one God" at Sexagesima). In the new translations: *through Thy beloved Son, Jesus Christ our Lord, who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.* Used at [13], [81], [92].
- **(b) Dietrich gives no conclusion, and the Son has already been named.** Revised TLHy: as in (a), e.g., [46]. New translations: *through the same, Thy beloved Son, Jesus Christ our Lord, who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.* Used at [6], [16], [23], [83], [89], [90], [94], [95], [96], [97], [98].
- **(c) Dietrich's last clause already contains "through Christ," "through Him" or "through Thy Son."** That phrase is moved to the end of its clause, as TLHy does at Christmas and Easter 2, and the conclusion follows it: "…through Him, *who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.*" Only the word order changes. Revised TLHy at [2], [3], [26], [34], [35], [37], [38], [40], [52], [56], [63], [64], [67], [70]; new translations at [8], [30], [79], [84], [85].
- **(d) Dietrich already closes with Christ.** His close is kept and continued, as TLHy does at Easter 1 and Trinity 8: "…through Jesus Christ, Thy Son and our Lord, *who liveth and reigneth…*". Revised TLHy at [5], [15], [27], [28], [32], [36], [42], [44], [53], [54], [61]; new translations at [29] ("for the sake of Thy dear Son and our Redeemer, Jesus Christ") and [91].
- **(e) Prayers to Christ.** *Thou who livest and reignest with the Father and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end.* This is TLHy's form at Trinity 5 ([51]). Used also at [93], and at [80], whose last petition is addressed to the Son.
- **(f) The prayer to the Holy Trinity, [45].** *who livest and reignest, one God, world without end.* This is the prayer-book ending for a prayer addressed to the Holy Trinity, as in the Book of Common Prayer's collect for Trinity Sunday. It reads "one God" where TLHy's other endings read "one true God"; the prayer-book wording is kept.
- **(g) Dietrich's own Trinitarian conclusion.** Nothing is added. Where TLHy had replaced or enlarged his close, Dietrich's words are restored ([18], [68], [71]); at Pentecost ([43]) TLHy's rendering of his close is accurate and is kept. At [86]/[87] the prayer closes "…through Him live eternally with Thee and Thy Holy Ghost, and be saved."

The two appended prayers in Part III end with their own conclusions, and nothing is added to them.

### 2.5 How each entry is laid out

1. **Heading:** Stratman's number and the day in TLHy's calendar.
2. **Dietrich's heading:** the section heading in the *Summaria*, with an English rendering in brackets.
3. **German:** the prayer as printed, with its PDF page.
4. **Revised TLHy** (TLHy's collect, corrected to the German) or **New translation** (where TLHy lacks the prayer).
5. **Congregational form**, where Dietrich prays in the first person singular.
6. **Changes from TLHy** (revised texts) or **Notes** (new translations).

Stratman's modern versions are compared with the German in Appendix C.

### Dedicatory letter to Johann Neudörffer (PDF pp. 5–12)

The letter runs on without a break in the print. The paragraph divisions (¶1–¶10) are editorial and are the same in the German and the English. Each paragraph gives the German first (indented) and the translation after it. [p. 6] and the like mark where a new PDF page begins.

**Address (PDF p. 5)**

> Dem Ersamen/vnd namhafften Johann Newdörffer/deutscher Schulmeyster in Nürmberg/Meinem sonder lieben freunde.

To the honorable and renowned Johann Neudörffer, German schoolmaster in Nuremberg, my especially dear friend.

**¶1** (p. 5)

> DEr heylige Simeon/da er zu Jerusalem im Tempel/das kindlin Jesus in seinen armē hat/weyssagt er/Es werde nicht yederman diß kindlin annemen/noch für seinen heyland haben/sonder der meyste theyl/werde jm widersprechen/Vnd sein lere/so die eynige rechte seligkeyt ist/lestern/vnnd alß Ketzerey verdammen/Ob nun solches den Christen sehr werde wehe thun/soll es doch dazu dienen/das viler hertzen gedancken offenbar werden/vnnd wir die leut recht erkennen lernen/vnd durch falschen scheyn/nicht betrogen werden.

Holy Simeon, when he has the child Jesus in his arms in the Temple at Jerusalem, prophesies that not everyone will receive this child or hold Him for his Savior, but that the greater part will speak against Him, and will blaspheme His doctrine, which is the one true salvation, and condemn it as heresy. Now although this will grieve Christians sorely, it shall nevertheless serve to this end: that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed, and that we may learn to know people rightly and not be deceived by a false appearance.

**¶2** (pp. 6–7)

> [p. 6] Solche weyssagung gehet für vnnd für vnter den Christen/Bald/das liebe Euangelion sich lest hören/ist es allenthalb vol zungen/die jhm widersprechen/vnd alß Ketzerey vnd verfürische lehr es verdammen/Ja es ist an dem widersprechen nit gnug/man nimbt auch das schwert in die faust/vñ wil es mit gewalt dempffen. Werden derhalb die armen Christen mit zweyen schwerten geschlagen/Das eine durchdringet jnen jr hertz/Denn es ye einen grossen grewlichen schmertzen geberē muß/das die welt so blind ist/den gnedigen Gott im himel so lestert/dem Teuffel so willig diener/dem wort/da doch all jr seligkeyt anligt/so bitter feynd/vnnd der lügen/dem ergsten seelengifft/so vber alle massen zugethon vnnd hold ist/Das ander schwert/Nemlich/die verfolgung/mattet/verwundet/vnd würget den leyb/Solches soll mit disem kind/mit seim wort/dem heyligen Euangelio/vnd darnach mit allen Christen/für vnd für practiciert vnd geübt werden.

This prophecy comes to pass continually among Christians. As soon as the dear gospel makes itself heard, everywhere is full of tongues that speak against it and condemn it as heresy and seductive doctrine. Indeed, the speaking against is not enough; men also take the sword in hand and would stifle it by force. Poor Christians are therefore struck with two swords. The one pierces through their heart. For it must surely bring forth a great and dreadful pain that the world is so blind, so blasphemes the gracious God in heaven, is so willing a servant to the devil, so bitter an enemy to the word, on which all its salvation yet depends, and is so devoted and attached beyond all measure to the lie, the worst poison of souls. The other sword, namely persecution, wearies, wounds, and slays the body. This shall be practiced and carried out continually upon this child, upon His word, the holy gospel, and thereafter upon all Christians.

**¶3** (p. 7)

> [p. 7] Was aber für ein ergetzligkeit in yenem leben werde folgen/wissen wir auß Gottes wort lautter vnd gewiß/deñ es wirt ein vberauß herrlich/frölich/vnd wonreychs wort sein/wenn der Son Gottes sagen wirt: Kombt her jr gesegneten meines Vatters/ererbet das Reych/das euch bereytet ist/von anbegin der welt. Die hunde aber/vnnd die Zauberer(wie inn der Offenbarung am 22. stehet) Vnd die Hurer/vnd die Todschleger/vnd die Abgöttischen/vnd alle die lieb haben vnd thun die lügen/werden hinunder müssen inn das hellische fewer/vnnd ewige flammen/Da wirt sich alß denn der rechte wechssel finden.

But what delight will follow in the life beyond, we know clearly and certainly from God's word. For it will be an exceedingly glorious, joyful, and blissful word when the Son of God shall say: "Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world." But the dogs, and the sorcerers (as it stands in Revelation 22), and the whoremongers, and the murderers, and the idolaters, and all who love and make a lie, will have to go down into the fire of hell and the eternal flames. There, then, the right exchange will be found.

**¶4** (pp. 7–8)

> Hie aber auff erden/sagt Simeon/sollen die Feynde des worts offenbar werden/Das alle Christen jhnen in das hertz hinein sehen/vnd gründlich sie vrteylen können/wer sie sind/wie sie leben vnd sterben/vnd was für ein vrtheyl sie für Gott in ewigkeyt tragen müssen/Solchs ist nicht ein geringes/damit die Christen inn der verfolgung [p. 8] sich mögē trösten.

But here on earth, says Simeon, the enemies of the word shall be revealed, so that all Christians can see into their heart and judge them thoroughly: who they are, how they live and die, and what judgment they must bear before God in eternity. That is no small thing, with which Christians may comfort themselves in persecution.

**¶5** (p. 8)

> Wir haben in deutschen land nun ein lange zeyt das widersprechen/starck hören vnd dulden müssen/Yetzundt kombt das schwert mit gewalt dazu/das der Antichrist zu Rom/mit seim Anhang vnsere kirchen gar will tilgen/vnd verwüsten/vnd durch sein Antichristisch Conciliabel zu Trient/alle abgötterey vnd lügen wider anrichten. Solches fürnemen/schmucken sie zierlich vnnd fein/vnd ist gewiß/wer jhren worten will glauben/der ist schon betrogen. Denn anderst hörest nicht von jnen/denn das sie die strittigen Religion/vergleychen/eynigkeyt anrichten/vñ die Scheflein in den rechten stal bringen wöllen.

We in the German land have now for a long time had to hear and endure the speaking against in full force. Now the sword comes with force besides, in that the Antichrist at Rome, with his adherents, would utterly destroy and lay waste our churches, and through his antichristian conciliabulum at Trent set up again all idolatry and lies. This undertaking they adorn elegantly and finely, and it is certain that whoever will believe their words is already deceived. For you hear nothing else from them than that they would settle the disputed religion, establish unity, and bring the little sheep into the right fold.

**¶6** (pp. 8–9)

> Vnd kan wol sein/das jr vil/so zu solchem blutdürstigen fürnemen helffen/jres wandels vnd lebens halb from̃ sind/Aber was saget Simeon/bald du das widersprechen hörest/das sie des Herrn Christi wort nicht wöllen leyden/noch war lassen sein/so frag nicht weyter/jr hertz stehet dir so glat vnd gar offen/alß hettest [p. 9] du es in deiner hand/Kanst derhalb vrtheylen/das es eytel Heucheley mit jnen ist/das der Teuffel jhnen im hertzen sitzt/sie treybet vnd füret/Vnd in summa/das sie gar des Teuffels sind/wie sie stehen vnd gehen/vnangesehen jres ampts/stands/wandels/zucht/vñ was sonst mehr für tugend an jnen mögen gerhümet werden. Denn das Euangelion verfolgen/den Son Gottes nicht allein für vnsern erlöser halten/Andere mittel der seligkeyt den leuten fürhalten/vnd suchen/Abgötterey verteydigen ꝛc. solches ist ein sünde/die nicht allein hie auff erden mit allerley plagen/sonder in yener welt/mit dem hellischen ewigen fewer/muß gestraffet werden.

And it may well be that many of those who help in such a bloodthirsty undertaking are upright as regards their conduct and life. But what does Simeon say? As soon as you hear the speaking against, that they will not suffer the word of the Lord Christ nor let it be true, then ask no further: their heart stands as plainly and wide open to you as if you had it in your hand. You can therefore judge that it is sheer hypocrisy with them, that the devil sits in their heart, drives and leads them, and, in sum, that they are wholly the devil's, as they stand and go, without regard to their office, station, conduct, discipline, and whatever other virtue may be praised in them. For to persecute the gospel, not to hold the Son of God alone for our Redeemer, to set other means of salvation before people and to seek them, to defend idolatry, etc.: this is a sin which must be punished not only here on earth with all manner of plagues, but in the world beyond with the eternal fire of hell.

**¶7** (pp. 9–10)

> Da sehen wir/die wir sonst des Creutzes/vnd der verfolgung halben/kleinmütig vnnd verzagt sind/was für einen grossen treffenlichen vortheyl/wir für der welt haben/ob gleich die selb mit gewalt/ehren/gut/vnd anders vns vberlegē ist. Sollen derhalb dem genedigen Gott im Hymel/ [p. 10] nicht vndanckbar für solche gnade sein/vnd sonderlich weyl die welt/mit dem widersprechen nicht will ablassen/sonder noch das schwert dazu in die faust nimbt/sollen wir beden/dem Teuffel vnnd der welt zu trotz/Vnnd darnach dem Son Gottes zu ehren/vnnd vnseren nachkommen zu nutz/mit dem bekentniß in alle weg/so vns müglich/getrewlich vnd fleyssig anhalten/vnd vnsere rechte Christenliche lehr/auff alle weyse zu pflantzen/vnd erweytern versuchen.

Here we, who are otherwise fainthearted and despondent on account of the cross and of persecution, see what a great and excellent advantage we have over the world, although it is superior to us in power, honors, wealth, and other things. We should therefore not be unthankful to the gracious God in heaven for such grace. And especially, since the world will not leave off the speaking against, but takes the sword in hand besides, we should, in defiance of both the devil and the world, and further to the honor of the Son of God and for the good of our descendants, persevere faithfully and diligently in the confession in every way possible to us, and try in every manner to plant and extend our true Christian doctrine.

**¶8** (pp. 10–11)

> Sonderlich aber sollen wir auff das junge volck sehen/das es inn zeyt zu Gottes wort gehalten/vnnd trewlich damit vnterrichtet werde. Solche vrsach hat mich bewegt/dise kurtze Euangelien Summaria/so ich für meine kindlein zuschreyben newlich angefangen/yetzt diser zeyt/da ich schwachheyt halb/mein ampt in der Kirch/nicht hab selb verwalten können/gar zuuerfertigen/vnnd der jugend zu gut inn druck zu geben/auff das/wer lust dazu hat/meiner arbeyt [p. 11] möchte geniessen/ob ich gleych stetter kranckheyt halb/das predigampt hab müssen anstehen lassen.

But especially we should look to the young people, that they be kept to God's word early and faithfully instructed in it. This reason has moved me, now at this time when on account of weakness I have not been able to administer my office in the church myself, to finish completely these short summaries of the Gospels, which I recently began to write for my little children, and to give them to the press for the good of the youth, so that whoever has a desire for it might have the benefit of my labor, although on account of continual illness I have had to let the office of preaching rest.

**¶9** (p. 11)

> Jch hab aber günstiger vnnd lieber Newdörffer/euch vnd ewer schule/damit verehren wöllen/weyl jr/alß ein Christlicher trewer Schulmeyster/ewr gesindlin sonderlich fleyssig zu Gottes wort haltet/das ich nicht allein zu solchem gutem werck euch hülffe/sonder durch ewr Exempel/auch andere deudsche vnnd lateynische Schulmeyster/reytzete/ewrem Exempel nach/hie vnd an andern orten/die jugend fleyssig zu Gottes wort zu halten. Denn da müssen wir anheben/so wir anders Christliche lehr erhalten/vnnd Gottes namen heyligen/Ja auch fromme/gefölgige/züchtige/vnd glückselige Kinder haben wöllen.

But, kind and dear Neudörffer, I have wished to honor you and your school with it, because you, as a Christian and faithful schoolmaster, keep your little household especially diligently to God's word; so that I might not only help you in such a good work, but through your example might also stir up other German and Latin schoolmasters, after your example, here and in other places, to keep the youth diligently to God's word. For there we must begin, if indeed we would preserve Christian doctrine and hallow God's name, yes, and also have godly, obedient, well-disciplined, and happy children.

**¶10** (pp. 11–12)

> Bey den Papisten soll diß klein Büchlein/so vnansehenlich es ist/sein frucht auch reychlich mit bringen/das es zum ewigen zeuckniß/sampt andern der vnsern schrifften offentlich da stehen sol/das sie vñ yederman sehen/was doch [p. 12] mangels an vnser lehr sey/die sie bißher mit lestern so hart angefochten/vnd yetzundt mit dem schwert gar zu tilgen sich vnterfangen haben. Aber sie sollens nicht enden/Dise lehr/soll vnd muß bleyben/Sie aber/wenn sie es nit wöllen leyden/müssen drüber zu grund gehen/vñ soll sie kein macht noch herrligkeyt helffen. Der Allmechtige/wölle euch/vnd alle fromme Christen/sonderlich zu diser kömmerlichen zeyt/gnedig erhalten vnd behüten/Amen.

Among the papists too this little book, unimpressive as it is, shall bring its fruit abundantly, in that it shall stand publicly, together with other writings of ours, as an everlasting testimony, so that they and everyone may see what fault, after all, there is in our doctrine, which they have hitherto assailed so hard with blasphemy and have now undertaken to destroy utterly with the sword. But they shall not accomplish it. This doctrine shall and must remain. But they, if they will not suffer it, must perish over it, and no power or glory shall help them. May the Almighty graciously preserve and protect you and all godly Christians, especially in this sorrowful time. Amen.

**Date and subscription (p. 12)**

> Geschriben den andern September/in meiner studier vnd siech stube. Anno 1546.
>
> E. W. Vitus Dietrich.

Written on the second of September, in my study and sickroom, in the year 1546.

Your willing [servant], Vitus Dietrich.

**Notes**
- **Neudörffer (Address).** Johann Neudörffer the Elder (1497–1563) was Nuremberg's celebrated writing and arithmetic master. *deutscher Schulmeyster* means the master of a German school, one that taught in German rather than in Latin; Dietrich draws the same distinction in ¶9 ("German and Latin schoolmasters"). *sonder lieben* is "especially dear."
- **Simeon (¶1, ¶4).** The letter is built on Luke 2:34–35. *widersprechen* ("speak against") is the verb of Luke 2:34 in Luther's Bible ("ein Zeichen, dem widersprochen wird"; King James: "a sign which shall be spoken against"), and the noun *das widersprechen* is rendered "the speaking against" throughout. "That the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed" is Luke 2:35 in King James wording, which Dietrich's German follows closely.
- **The two swords (¶2).** The first sword "pierces through" the heart, applying to all Christians Simeon's word to Mary, "a sword shall pierce through thy own soul also" (Luke 2:35). The second is persecution.
- **"So willing a servant to the devil" (¶2).** The print reads *diener* ("servant"), checked at 400 dpi. *dienet* ("serves") would also make sense, but the noun matches the parallel *feynd* ("enemy") in the next clause.
- **Scripture in ¶3.** The words of the Son of God are Matthew 25:34. The list of the condemned is Revelation 22:15, in King James wording; the rest of that sentence, that they "must go down into the fire of hell," is Dietrich's own (compare Revelation 21:8).
- **"The right exchange" (¶3).** *wechssel* means exchange or reversal: in the life beyond, present fortunes are turned around.
- **Trent (¶5).** The Council of Trent had opened in December 1545. *Conciliabel* (Latin *conciliabulum*) is the term for an illegitimate council; Dietrich denies Trent the name of a council. *vergleychen* ("settle") is the vocabulary of the religious colloquies that sought to reconcile the two parties; Dietrich had himself taken part in the one held at Regensburg in 1546.
- **The sword "now" (¶5, ¶7, ¶10).** The letter is dated 2 September 1546, during the Schmalkaldic War, which had broken out that summer. This is the sword that, Dietrich says, has now been added to the speaking against.
- **"As they stand and go" (¶6).** An idiom for "in all that they are and do."
- **Dietrich's illness (¶8, subscription).** He says he can neither administer his office nor preach, because of continual illness, and dates the letter from his "study and sickroom." He died at Nuremberg on 25 March 1549.
- **"Honor you with it" (¶9).** *verehren* here has its older sense of honoring someone with a gift, that is, presenting the book to him.
- **"Your little household" (¶9).** *gesindlin* is the diminutive of *Gesinde*, a household; here it evidently means Neudörffer's pupils.
- **Subscription.** *E. W.* is taken as the usual abbreviation for *Ewer Williger*, "your willing [servant]."
- **Two-word forms (¶7).** *ob gleich* ("although") and *die selb* ("the same") are printed as two words and are kept so.

### Appended prayer 1. For children in perilous times (pp. 299–300)

**Heading (PDF p. 299):** *Wie die Eltern jhre kindlin zu disen fehrlichen zeyten/vmb erhaltung des wort Gotes/wider die feinde des heyligen Euangelij/sollen lernen betten.* [How parents should teach their little children to pray, in these perilous times, for the preservation of God's word against the enemies of the holy gospel.]

> HErr Got himlischer Vater/wir dancken dir/dz du vns das selige liecht deines wortes so gnediglich angezündet/vñ biß her hast lassen leuchten/vnd bitten dich/Du wöllest ja zu diser zeyt ob solchem liecht gnediglich halten/vnd den Sathan vnd der argen welt nit gestatten/das sie es außleschen. **Translation**

> Lord God, heavenly Father, we thank Thee that Thou hast so graciously kindled for us the blessed light of Thy word, and hast suffered it to shine until now; and we beseech Thee, do Thou at this time graciously uphold this light, and suffer not Satan and the wicked world to quench it. > Du wöllest auch vmb deiner Ehre willen/fürthin deine lere vnd Christliche [final e damaged] selige regiment/in disen Landen gnediglich erhalten/vñ vnsere Herrschafft mit deinem heyligen Geyst/zu Christlicher seliger regierung neigen/das sie vnd die Vnterthan/in rechter erkentnus Jesu Christi/in rechter anrüffung vñ gehorsam gegen dir/vnd in frieden leben vnd bleiben/vnd dich ewiglich preisen vnd dir dancken. **Translation**

> True, eternal, and almighty God, Father of our Savior Jesus Christ, Creator of heaven and earth, together with Thine eternal Son and Holy Ghost: We confess that, alas, we have much sin, and have sinned not only unwittingly but also wittingly, with great ingratitude, and have well deserved Thy wrath and punishment; and we have a hearty displeasure at our sins.
>
> But we pray from our hearts that Thou wouldest graciously forgive us all our sins for the sake of Thy dear Son, Jesus Christ, whom Thou hast appointed to be the Reconciler, and govern our hearts with Thy Holy Ghost, that we may live before Thee at all times in true faith, true invocation, and true obedience.
>
> And since Thou seest the great need of our rulers and of us all, men, women, and children, and that without Thy help we have no protection; since Thou knowest also the hearts of all men, and seest that our enemies chiefly seek the destruction of true doctrine and the setting up and establishing of their shameful idolatry and lewdness: we therefore beseech Thee from our hearts, through our Savior Jesus Christ, that for Thine honor's sake Thou wouldest graciously protect and keep, now and at all times, our rulers, our church, ourselves, our children, and our households, as Thou didst preserve and protect Thy people Israel in the Red Sea; and that Thou wouldest destroy the counsel and might of the enemies, and not suffer the murderous foreign nation to practice its lewdness and cruelty upon our wives and children.
>
> Do Thou also, for Thine honor's sake, henceforth graciously preserve Thy doctrine and Christian and blessed government in these lands, and by Thy Holy Ghost incline our rulers to Christian and blessed rule, that they and the subjects may live and abide in the true knowledge of Jesus Christ, in true invocation of Thee and obedience toward Thee, and in peace, and may praise Thee and give thanks unto Thee forever. Thou hast said: "Call upon Me in trouble, and I will hear thee, that thou shalt glorify Me." Such words of Thine are without doubt true. **Notes**
- **Psalm 50:15.** Bugenhagen quotes the verse with *erhören* ("hear"), where Luther's Bible reads *erretten* ("deliver"). The translation follows Bugenhagen.
- **Rendering choices.**
  - *versöner* (*Versöhner*) is "Reconciler."
  - *Herrschafft* is the ruling authority, here "our rulers."
  - *anrüffung* is invocation, the calling upon God.
  - *vnzucht* is unchastity or lewdness.
  - *heußlin* are little houses, that is, households.
- **Person.** The prayer speaks as "we" throughout but closes *Darumb bit ich dich* ("Therefore I beseech Thee"); this is kept as printed.
- **Paragraphs.** The paragraph breaks follow the print.
- **Setting (an inference).** The prayer asks protection for rulers, church and households against enemies bent on rooting out true doctrine and setting up idolatry, and against a "murderous foreign nation." In a book printed at Nuremberg in 1548, whose dedication (2 September 1546) speaks of the sword now added to the speaking against the gospel (front matter, ¶5), this points to the Schmalkaldic War of 1546–47, and the unnamed "foreign nation" is presumably the emperor's foreign soldiery. Bugenhagen addressed the Saxon clergy on that war in 1546 (*Von der jetzigen Kriegsrüstung*). Whether this prayer comes from that writing has not been checked.

## Appendix B. ELH collects in Stratman

Stratman marks with "(ELH)" the prayers that, in his words, "appear in Evangelical Lutheran Hymnary alone." There are four: [73], [75], [76] and [78]. None of them is Dietrich's, and the *Summaria* has no German for any of them. Each is given below with what could be found of its origin, with Stratman's text, and in a version in TLHy's idiom.

**What was found.**

- **[73] Last Sunday after Trinity** is a Latin Advent collect of the Old Gelasian Sacramentary, which came into English in William Bright's *Ancient Collects* (1862). ELH is the first Lutheran book found to have it. A new translation is made here from the Latin.
- **[76] Mission Festival** goes back to a collect first printed in 1975, in the Church of England's Series 3 collects and the British *Methodist Service Book*. *Lutheran Worship* (1982) recast it as its Collect of the Day for Mission Festival, and ELH took it from there. The version here is made from that Lutheran form.
- **[75] and [78] are in TLHy after all.** TLHy prints two series of collects: "The first Series of Collects is to be used with the first Service; the second Series, together with the Introits, to be used with the second or Common Service" (p. 44). This edition follows the first series. The second series gives a further collect after each day's Introit. [75] is TLHy's second-series collect for the Day of Humiliation and Prayer (p. 81), and [78] its collect for A Day of General or Special Thanksgiving (p. 82). Both came to TLHy from the Common Service of 1888, and ELH follows TLHy's wording of them. Stratman marks the harvest collect [77] (Appendix A), which stands in the same series on the same page, as TLHy's (H); [75] and [78] belong with it. For these two, TLHy's own text is the version in TLHy's idiom.

**How the entries are given.** Each entry gives the source text where there is one, Stratman's text with its ELH number, the version in TLHy's idiom, and notes. Stratman's texts are quoted from his PDF (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). Where ELH's own wording is quoted, it comes from search excerpts of the Internet Archive's scan of ELH; the rest of ELH's text could not be consulted, and Stratman's text stands in for it. The versions follow §2.2–2.4, with "Holy Ghost" for "Holy Spirit" and TLHy's conclusions. Because none of these prayers is Dietrich's, nothing is set in italics.

### [73] Last Sunday after Trinity

**Placement.** Stratman: Last Sunday after Trinity. ELH: no. 136. TLHy has no collect of its own for this day. At the Twenty-seventh Sunday after Trinity it directs: "(The Collect for the Sixth Sunday after Epiphany shall be used on the last Sunday after Trinity in each year)" (p. 81), that is, [20] (Appendix A).

**Latin (Old Gelasian Sacramentary, Mohlberg no. 1128)**

> Fac nos, quaesumus, domine deus noster, peruigiles atque sollicitos aduentum expectare Christi filii tui domini nostri, ut dum uenerit pulsans, non dormientis peccatis sed uigilantes et in suis inueniat laudibus exultantes: per.

**Stratman (from ELH no. 136)**

> Lord God, heavenly Father, make us watchful and heedful in awaiting the coming of your Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, that when he stands at the door and knocks, he may find us not sleeping in carelessness and sin, but awake and rejoicing in his appearance; through your beloved Son, Jesus Christ, our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one true God, now and forever. Amen.

**Version in TLHy's idiom (new translation from the Latin)**

> Make us, we beseech Thee, O Lord our God, watchful and heedful in awaiting the coming of Thy Son, Christ our Lord, that when He cometh and knocketh, He may find us not sleeping in sins, but watching, and rejoicing in His praises; through the same, Thy beloved Son, Jesus Christ our Lord, who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end. Amen.

**Origin**
- **Latin.** The prayer is an Advent collect of the Old Gelasian Sacramentary (Vatican, Reg. lat. 316). It stands in the Mass headed *Item alia missa* (§LXXXI), one of the Advent Masses that follow the *Orationes de aduentum domini* (§LXXX), and is no. 1128 in Mohlberg's edition. It also closes the *Orationes de adventu Domini quotidianis diebus* [Prayers of the Lord's Advent for weekdays] in Ménard's edition of the Gregorian Sacramentary (Migne, *Patrologia Latina* 78, col. 194), and it is no. 2600 in the *Corpus Orationum*. The Roman Missal of Paul VI revised it as the collect for Monday of the First Week of Advent, with *orationibus vigilantes* ("watching in prayer") in place of *non dormientes peccatis sed vigilantes* ("not sleeping in sins, but watching").
- **Scripture.** *dum uenerit pulsans … inueniat … uigilantes* takes up Luke 12:36–37: "that when he cometh and knocketh, they may open unto him immediately. Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he cometh shall find watching." The Vulgate has the same verbs (*cum venerit et pulsaverit … invenerit vigilantes*).
- **English.** "Watchful and heedful in awaiting the Coming of Thy Son" is William Bright's rendering in *Ancient Collects* (2nd ed., 1862, p. 16), where the prayer follows three other Gelasian Advent collects (Mohlberg nos. 1120, 1125 and 1127). The sixth edition (1887, p. 16) reads: "Make us, we beseech Thee, O Lord our God, watchful and heedful in awaiting the Coming of Thy Son Christ our Lord; that when He shall come and knock, He may find us not sleeping in sins, but awake, and rejoicing in His praises; through the same Jesus Christ our Lord." From Bright the prayer passed into many Anglican and devotional collections.
- **ELH.** ELH's text opens "Lord God, heavenly Father, make us watchful and heedful in awaiting the coming of Your Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, that when He shall stand at the door and knock, He may find…". It is Bright's English, given the address of the Dietrich collects ("Lord God, heavenly Father") and the words of Revelation 3:20 ("Behold, I stand at the door, and knock"). No earlier Lutheran book has been found with the prayer. The Common Service (1888) and the *Church Book* (1892) give the Twenty-seventh Sunday after Trinity no collect of its own and point instead to the Introit and Collect of the Twenty-third Sunday after Trinity; TLHy (1913) points to the collect of the Sixth Sunday after Epiphany; and the *Common Service Book* (1917) gives "Absolve, we beseech Thee, O Lord, Thy people from their offences…". *The Lutheran Hymnal* (1941) could not be checked.
- **Stratman's website.** Stratman's posting of this prayer (*A Collection of Prayers*, 11 June 2020) gives its source as "Veit Dietrich, d. 1549, Trinity Last." It is not in the *Summaria*, which ends with the Twenty-fifth Sunday after Trinity, and his own PDF marks it (ELH).

**Notes**
- **Basis.** The version translates the Latin and keeps Bright's English wherever it renders the Latin without loss.
- **Differences from Stratman's (ELH's) text**
  - "Lord God, heavenly Father, make us" → "Make us, we beseech Thee, O Lord our God," restoring *domine deus noster* and *quaesumus*.
  - "your Son, our Lord Jesus Christ" → "Thy Son, Christ our Lord" (*Christi filii tui domini nostri*).
  - "when he stands at the door and knocks" (ELH: "when He shall stand at the door and knock") → "when He cometh and knocketh" (*dum uenerit pulsans*), in the King James words of Luke 12:36.
  - "not sleeping in carelessness and sin" → "not sleeping in sins" (*non dormientis peccatis*).
  - "awake" → "watching" (*uigilantes*), as in Luke 12:37, "shall find watching."
  - "rejoicing in his appearance" → "rejoicing in His praises" (*in suis … laudibus exultantes*). Stratman's website has "appearing."
- **Conclusion.** The Latin closes *per* ("through…"), and the Son has already been named, so the conclusion is that of §2.4 (b), as in Bright's "through the same Jesus Christ our Lord."
- **Spelling.** Mohlberg prints the manuscript's spellings: *peruigiles*, *aduentum*, *uenerit* and *uigilantes* (= *pervigiles*, *adventum*, *venerit*, *vigilantes*), and *dormientis* for *dormientes*.

### [76] Mission Festival

**Placement.** Stratman: Mission Festival. ELH: no. 140 (Mission Festival). TLHy has no Mission Festival.

**English original (1975)**

> Almighty God, who called your Church to witness that you were in Christ reconciling men to yourself: help us so to proclaim the good news of your love, that all who hear it may be reconciled to you; through him who died for us …

**Stratman (from ELH no. 140)**

> Almighty God, since you have called your Church to witness that in Christ you reconciled us to yourself, grant that by your Holy Spirit we may proclaim the good news of your salvation, that all who hear it may receive the gift of salvation; through Jesus Christ, your Son, our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one true God, now and forever. **Version in TLHy's idiom**

> Almighty God, who hast called Thy Church to witness that in Christ Thou hast reconciled us to Thyself: Grant, we beseech Thee, that by Thy Holy Ghost we may proclaim the gospel of Thy salvation, that all who hear it may receive the gift of salvation; through Jesus Christ, Thy Son, our Lord, who liveth and reigneth with Thee and the Holy Ghost, one true God, world without end. Amen.

**Origin**
- **1975.** The prayer first appears in two British books of 1975: the Church of England Liturgical Commission's *Collects to Accompany the Lectionary for Holy Communion, Series 3*, and the *Methodist Service Book*. It rests on 2 Corinthians 5:18–19: "God, who hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation; to wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself." Later books read "reconciling the world to yourself."
- ***Lutheran Worship* (1982).** LW recast it as the Collect of the Day for Mission Festival: "Almighty God, since you have called your Church to witness that in Christ you reconciled us to yourself, grant that by your Holy Spirit…". Where the 1975 prayer has "you were in Christ reconciling" (verse 19), the Lutheran speaks of the finished act, "in Christ you reconciled us to yourself" (verse 18). For "the good news of your love" it has "the good news of your salvation." And where the 1975 prayer asks that all who hear "may be reconciled to you," the Lutheran asks that they "may receive the gift of salvation." LW also prints the prayer, with "you have reconciled," among its prayers, under "Missionary work."
- **ELH.** ELH no. 140 is LW's collect ("Almighty God, since You have called Your Church to witness that in Christ You reconciled…"), and Stratman's text agrees with it.
- **Later.** *Lutheran Service Book* (2006) prints a shortened form among its prayers, "For the mission of the Church" (no. 104): "Almighty God, You have called Your Church to witness that in Christ You have reconciled us to Yourself. Grant that…".

**Notes**
- **Basis.** The version renders the Lutheran form (LW, ELH), which is the prayer that ELH and Stratman print. The 1975 original differs from it in substance (above) and is not followed.
- **Collect shape.** "Since you have called…, grant that…" becomes TLHy's relative clause and petition: "who hast called…: Grant, we beseech Thee, that…" (§2.2).
- **Scripture.** "Thou hast reconciled us to Thyself" follows 2 Corinthians 5:18, "who hath reconciled us to himself." "The gospel of Thy salvation" gives "the good news of your salvation" in the words of Ephesians 1:13, "the gospel of your salvation." TLHy's collects never say "good news"; they speak of "the gospel of Thy Son" and "the light of Thy gospel."
- **Holy Ghost.** "By your Holy Spirit" → "by Thy Holy Ghost" (§2.3).
- **Conclusion.** ELH already closes "through Jesus Christ, your Son, our Lord." That close is kept and continued in TLHy's form, as in §2.4 (d).

## Appendix C. Stratman compared

Paul C. Stratman's *The Collects of Veit Dietrich* gives every collect in modern English. It was used throughout as a check on accuracy, never as a source of wording.

**Where TLHy has the collect.** Stratman's text is a modernization of TLHy. Thee/Thou becomes you, relative clauses become statements, "We beseech Thee" is usually dropped before the imperative, and long sentences are divided. The conclusion becomes "who lives and reigns … now and forever," and the third Person is named in the modern form throughout. "Quicken" becomes "awaken," "carnal security" becomes "worldly security," "covetousness" becomes "greed," "Thy holy word" becomes "your precious Word," and "became man" becomes "became fully human." "The Jews" becomes "your people long ago," and "the darkness of Papacy" becomes "the darkness of error." Because these texts follow TLHy, they share TLHy's departures from the German; those departures are listed under "Changes from TLHy" in each entry and are not repeated here.

**Where TLHy lacks the collect.** Stratman's texts are his own fresh translations in the same modern idiom. Each was checked clause by clause against the German. Most are sound paraphrases; his substantive departures are listed below, and the German has been followed in every case. The most consequential are these:
- [6]: *verfolgen* ("persecute") is taken as "do not follow."
- [8]: the subject and sense of the central clauses are reversed.
- [23]: the blind man's sight is transferred to "us."
- [29]: a causal clause is turned into a petition.
- [32] and [84]: *dauon ablassen* ("forsake them") is taken as "receive forgiveness."
- [83]: the petition against the tyrants is recast as a prayer for rulers' goodwill.
- [96]: *sich … versehen* ("look to [God] for") is taken as "share with others."
- [97]: the central image, the angels' hedge, is replaced.

**[10].** Stratman prints this prayer twice in modern dress, as [10] (Sunday after New Year, from the *Summaria*) and [24] (Quinquagesima, from TLHy), following TLHy's wording both times.

**[6] First Sunday of Christmas (Sunday after Christmas)**

- He reads *die so dein wort verfolgen* as "those who do not follow your Word." *verfolgen* means "persecute."
- He drops "rise again in Him" and "stumble at Him."
- He gives "rightly confess" for *recht erkennen* ("rightly know").
- He merges two separate clauses into "not be blinded by the false security of the world or by sufferings and afflictions."

**[8] The Circumcision / New Year's Day**

- He makes the Son the subject ("your Son put himself under the law").
- He renders the comfort clause "comfort despite such disobedience." The German speaks of Christ's obedience as our comfort.
- He gives "remain your obedient children" where the German has *anfahen*, "begin to be" obedient children.

**[13] First Sunday after Epiphany**

- "reached out to me with the blessing of baptism" is a paraphrase.
- He omits "and will."
- He gives "not forget" for *versaume* ("neglect").
- "May he dwell in my heart" is not in the German.
- He omits the closing "Amen," which the German has.

**[16] Third Sunday after Epiphany**

- He has "We have your Word"; the German says *His* word.
- He omits "with a word."
- He gives "remain in the faith until our last hour" for "at our last end abide firmly thereby."

**[23] Quinquagesima**

- He transfers the light from the blind man to "us" ("have let us poor sinners see your light").
- He omits "by Thy dear word."
- He makes Christ the object of knowledge; the German has "learn to know *Thee* through Christ."
- He gives "was counted guilty of our sins" for *für vnsere sünde bezalet* ("paid for our sins").
- He drops "sin" from "devil, sin, and death" and adds "finally be delivered in death."

**[29] Fifth Sunday in Lent (Judica)**

- He gives "remains in my word" for *helt* ("keeps").
- He turns the causal clause into a petition ("that your Son's Word may come to us and that we may hear it").
- He gives "never see eternal death" for *den todt in ewigkeyt nicht sehen*.

**[30] Palm Sunday**

- He gives "find our highest comfort" for "heartily comfort ourselves."
- He gives "Protect us from temptations to sin more" for "guard ourselves henceforth against sin."
- He gives "whatever hardships may come" for "what Thou sendest us to suffer."
- He gives "have eternal life" for "live with Thee eternally."
- His text has a typo, "you only Son."

**[32] Maundy Thursday**

- He gives "great mercy" for *grosse gnade*.
- He gives "confess our sins and receive forgiveness from it" for *vnsere sünde erkennen/vnd dauon ablassen* ("acknowledge our sins and forsake them").
- He gives "receive forgiveness of sins, hope and faith" for "through Christ hope for and believe the forgiveness of the same."

**[45] Festival of the Holy Trinity, first prayer**

- "created us at the beginning" misreads *erstlich* and omits *zu menschen*.
- He gives "bore the cross" for "paid for our sins upon the cross."
- His ending, "keep us … until our life's end in the eternal blessedness in the life to come," is reworked; the German has "keep us in Thy grace unto the end, and save us eternally."

**[79] St. Andrew, Apostle (November 30)**

- He omits "with Saint Andrew."
- He gives "in no way forsake him," which drops the persecution clause.
- He gives "until we are eternally blessed in him" for "until through Him we be wholly saved."

**[80] St. Thomas, Apostle (December 21)**

- He puts the whole prayer in the plural.
- He gives "through your Son you have given us comfort and help."
- He gives "not be overcome by unbelief, but strengthened in faithfulness to your Word" for "resist unbelief, and with entire trust commit myself to Thy word."

**[81] St. Stephen, Martyr (December 26)**

- He gives "by it he showed."
- He gives "finds its fullness in the next life" for "belongeth to another life."
- The conditional "if we also must suffer…" becomes "so that we can suffer any hardship."
- He omits "in place of the temporal."

**[83] The Holy Innocents (December 28)**

- He recasts the central petition: "Defend us from all harmful tyrants, all misfortune, so that rulers may have kind regard for your Christian Church, receive your blessings…". The German asks God to restrain the tyrants and turn the harm they plan against the Church upon their own heads. Nothing about rulers' goodwill is present.

**[84] The Conversion of St. Paul (January 25)**

- He puts the whole prayer in the plural.
- He gives "to be a follower and a faithful witness" for "a chosen vessel."
- He gives "receive forgiveness" for *dauon ablasse* ("forsake them"), the same misreading as at [32].
- He gives "humble sorrow" for *hertzliche rew* ("hearty contrition").

**[85] The Purification of Mary / Presentation of Our Lord (February 2)**

- He conforms the phrase to the biblical "glory of your people Israel."
- He omits "help" (*hilff vnd schutz*).

**[86] St. Matthias, Apostle (February 24)**

- His identical [86] and [87], including "Amen. Amen.", faithfully reproduce this edition.

**[87] The Annunciation (March 25)**

- He gives "bestowed on us poor sinners by sending your Son" for "been mindful of us poor sinners, and sent Thy Son."
- He gives "for our sake became fully human"; in the German the Father suffers the Son to become man.
- He omits "through Him" before "live eternally."

**[89] Sts. Philip and James, Apostles (May 1)**

- He makes the Son the thing shown ("for showing us your Son Christ Jesus so clearly"). The German says the Father's gracious will and loving heart are shown *in* the Son.
- He gives "overcome with hope" for "through such hope overcome all things."
- He omits "Amen."

**[90] The Nativity of St. John the Baptist (June 24)**

- He gives "humbly thank" for *von hertzen* ("from our hearts").
- He gives "great mercy" for *grosse gnad*.
- He omits "with his finger" and "without fear."

**[91] Sts. Peter and Paul, Apostles (June 29)**

- He gives "at all times" for *auff allen seyten* ("on every side").
- He gives "seeks to keep such knowledge away from us" for "seeks to drive us from this knowledge."
- He omits "unto our end."

**[92] The Visitation (July 2)**

- He gives "be judged for sin" for "fall into … sin."
- He recasts the Marian clause as "As the virgin Mary sang, neither wisdom nor power earn your help, and you do not regard them," omitting "money" and "them that fear Thee not."

**[93] St. Mary Magdalene (July 22)**

- He gives "trust in your work" for *verdienst* ("merit").
- He gives "secure against sin and evil," omitting "conscience" (*böses gewissen*).

**[94] St. James the Elder, Apostle (July 25)**

- He gives "you gave up your only Son" for "didst not spare."
- He omits "such a Captain."
- He turns "we beseech Thy fathomless mercy, grant by Thy Holy Ghost that…" into a direct request, "in your boundless mercy," for the gift of the Spirit.
- His text has a typo, "with as strong faith."

**[95] St. Bartholomew, Apostle (August 24)**

- He gives "bring many" for *yederman* ("every man").
- He gives "not be deterred by greed, arrogance or Satan." In the German, Satan is the one who beguiles, by means of covetousness, pride and the like.

**[96] St. Matthew, Apostle and Evangelist (September 21)**

- He gives "share all grace through your Son Christ Jesus with others," which misreads *versehen*.
- He smooths the mixed number to "Awaken our hearts."

**[97] St. Michael and All Angels (September 29)**

- He gives "created" for *verordnet* ("ordained").
- He replaces the central clause (the confidence that the angels will make a strong, sure hedge about us) with "hold to you as our refuge, that we may always have your dear angels around us to serve and strengthen us."
- He gives "break us or bring us to shame" for "break in and do harm."
- He rearranges the final clause and omits "Amen."

**[98] Sts. Simon and Jude, Apostles (October 28)**

- He gives "despise the world and the devil's wrath" for "the wrath of the world and of the devil." Otherwise his text is faithful.

## Appendix D. Observations on the 1548 edition

1. **Misprinted Scripture references in headings.**
   - Second Sunday in Advent, "Luce am 22." for Luke 21 (p. 16; [2]).
   - Epiphany, "Luc. 2." for Matthew 2 (p. 43; [12]).
   - Trinity, "Matth. 4." for Matthew 3 (p. 136; [45]).
   - Third Sunday after Trinity, "Luc. 19." for Luke 15 (p. 149; [49]).
   - St. Matthias, "Matthei am 9." for Matthew 11 (p. 247; [86]).
   - On the First Sunday in Lent the reference is partly blotted, "Ma[tth]. 4." (p. 76; [25]).
2. **Headings without a Sunday or a reference.** The Septuagesima section is headed only by its parable, with no Sunday named (p. 67; [21]). The Monday after Pentecost has no Scripture reference (p. 132; [44]). Pentecost cites Joel 2 rather than its Gospel (p. 129; [43]).
3. **Duplicated prayer.** The St. Matthias prayer (pp. 250–251) is the Annunciation prayer (pp. 254–255), with a doubled "Amen/Amen." See [86].
4. **Doubled Amen.** The Third Sunday after Easter also ends "Amen/AMEN." (p. 115; [38]).
5. **Typesetting slips.**
   - p. 75: "völlest" for "wöllest" ([23]).
   - p. 80: "vom dem" for "von dem" in the heading ([26]).
   - p. 95: ")" for a virgule ([30]).
   - p. 152: "ANEN" for "AMEN" ([49]).
   - p. 198: "Gerst" for "Geyst" ([63]).
   - p. 216: "gekommen" for "genommen" ([68]).
   - p. 296: "inn sere" for "inn vnsere" ([98]).
   - The catchwords disagree with the following page at p. 262 ("geschicket" vs. "schicket") and p. 275 ("reichlich" vs. "reychlich").
6. **Order of sections.** St. Stephen sits in the temporale after Christmas. Mary Magdalene (pp. 267–271) is printed before the Visitation (pp. 272–276). The Magdalene heading states that the feast is kept on the Seventh Sunday after Trinity.
7. **Two Trinity sections.** There are two Trinity sections, each with its own prayer: the Baptism text, Matthew 3 ([45]), and the Nicodemus text, John 3 ([46], the one TLHy uses).
8. **Epiphany 2 (Cana, pp. 54–57).** The prayer is headed *Für die/so im Ehestand sind* [For those who are in the estate of marriage], is in the first person singular (with a shift to "wir" partway through), and is followed by a rubric for the unmarried, given under [15]. No further Dietrich prayer is missing for this Sunday.
9. **End matter.** The book closes with Dietrich's address to the reader (pp. 297–298), the two appended prayers (pp. 299–303; Part III), and the colophon (p. 304). The colophon carries a woodcut of the Transfiguration with the banderole DOMINE BONVM EST NOS HIC ESSE (Matthew 17:4), the line *Psalm. LXXXIX. Wol dem Volck das jauchzen kan.* [Psalm 89: Blessed is the people that can rejoice] (Psalm 89:16 in Luther's numbering), and the printers' names.
10. **Dates.** The dedication is dated 2 September 1546 (p. 12), two years before this printing (1548). Stratman gives 1546 as the year the work was first published. If so, this is a later printing, and an earlier one would be the natural place to check the Matthias duplication (item 3) and the misprints above.
